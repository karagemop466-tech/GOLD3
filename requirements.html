The ten requirements and what the pass found

#Requirement & test applied GoodPartialFailUnverified Authority & what to do if it is missing
1Karat / purity statedThe listing must print the karat (10K, 14K, 18K …). A karat in an image or in another seller's policy does not count for this column.6740016 CFR Part 23 (FTC gold-marking rule)Ask the seller in writing which karat the metal is and keep the reply with the order.
2Word “solid” usedThe listing, or the seller's own materials policy, must say solid gold. Plated, vermeil, gold-filled, overlay and gold-tone do not qualify.19744116 CFR 23.4 (misuse of “gold”)Ask the seller to state “solid 14K gold” (or the karat bought) in writing before paying.
3Hallmark (karat stamp) disclosedThe seller should state that the piece is stamped with its karat. No seller in this file publishes a hallmark policy, so this column is almost always blank.3006816 CFR 23.9–23.10 (quality marks)Check the inside of the shank with a loupe the day the ring arrives.
4Maker's mark disclosedUnder 15 U.S.C. 297 a karat stamp must be accompanied by the manufacturer's or dealer's trademark or name. Two Dataset B listings document one.2006915 U.S.C. § 297Ask the seller whose registered mark sits beside the karat stamp.
5Finished weight in gramsA legitimate seller can state the ring's weight in grams. Ranges, stone-inclusive totals and client-side-only figures are marked partial, never treated as a gold weight.194048NIST SP 811 App. B.9 (the gram used here)Ask for the weight in grams; if refused, that is the answer.
6Independent seller reputationAn independent review sample (Trustpilot or BBB) plus working contact details. A seller with no recorded sample is left blank, not assumed good.4610015Trustpilot review profilesSearch the business name plus “complaints” and call the published number.
7Return window you can useNo US statute sets a minimum return window for a correctly delivered ring, so the window is the seller's promise. It must be long enough to have the ring independently tested; resized, engraved, custom and final-sale exclusions are shown where the seller states them.34289016 CFR Part 435 (FTC mail-order rule)Get the return window and exclusions in writing before ordering.
8Credit card acceptedA credit card gives Fair Credit Billing Act dispute rights. Bank wire, crypto and irreversible methods forfeit them; sellers that only advertise instalments are marked partial.292901315 U.S.C. § 1666 (Fair Credit Billing Act)Pay by credit card, or do not buy.
9Documentation capturedA verbatim price/description quote, the official URL it came from, and the date it was read. This is what you keep if the ring arrives wrong.7100015 U.S.C. § 1666 (keep the evidence for a dispute)Screenshot the listing and save the receipt before the page changes.
10Independent / XRF verificationWhether the seller offers, or will accept, an assay. Practically every seller states nothing, so the fallback is your own jeweller inside the return window.0706416 CFR Part 23 - a stamp is a marking, not an assayBook an XRF check with an independent jeweller before resizing or wearing.

What the matrix proves

  • No listing clears all ten requirements. 0 of 71 rows are ‘good’ on every column. The blockers are structural, not cosmetic: only 3 rows publish a hallmark, 2 publish a maker's mark, and no seller states an XRF or assay offer.
  • The seller can fix four of the ten himself. 6 rows are ‘good’ on karat, the word “solid”, a gram weight and a usable return window at the same time; 11 reach at least ‘partial’. Those four are published facts, so any seller could supply them today.
  • Karat is universal, proof is not. Karat is stated on 67 of 71 rows, but the word “solid” is on 19, contradicted or absent on 41, and fails outright on 4 look-alikes. That gap is exactly why the stamp alone is not verification.
  • Weight is the scarcest seller-published number. 19 of 71 rows publish a gram weight and 4 publish only a range. Without a weight no melt comparison is possible — the remaining 48 rows are left blank rather than back-calculated from the price.
  • Where a weight exists, the multiple is wide. 17 rows can be priced against their own gold: 1.37× to 25.07× melt value at the dated anchor. Rows whose weight includes the stone are labelled “ceiling”: the true price-to-gold multiple is higher.
  • Payment is the quiet trap. 29 rows confirm card acceptance, 29 are partial (wire also offered, or only instalments advertised) and 13 publish nothing checkable. A credit card is your Fair Credit Billing Act dispute route, so treat a blank here as a reason to ask before paying.
  • Returns exist, but the useful ones are conditional. 34 rows carry a window that can hold an independent test, 28 are partial (short window, store credit, resized/final-sale exclusion) and 9 are final sale or non-returnable. Resizing before an XRF check can destroy the return right.
  • XRF is never offered in writing. 64 rows record no seller statement and 7 record a seller statement only (Wilson's “stamped for X karat”, Menē's own certificate). Book an independent check inside the return window — that is the only column a seller cannot self-certify.
