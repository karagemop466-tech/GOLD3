What "break-even weight" means. It is the grams of gold the asking price would buy at spot. It is not the ring's weight. If a $1,500 14K ring has a break-even weight of 19.9 g, that means the ring would have to weigh 19.9 g for its gold alone to be worth $1,500 - a typical 2–4 g ring contains far less, and the rest of the price is the stone, the labour and the margin.

The formula, in full

spot_per_gram = spot_usd_per_troy_oz ÷ 31.1034768

melt_per_gram(karat) = spot_per_gram × (karat ÷ 24)

gold_value = melt_per_gram(karat) × ring_weight_grams

break_even_weight = asking_price ÷ melt_per_gram(karat)

1 troy ounce = 31.1034768 g exactly. Fineness = karat ÷ 24, so 14K = 58.333% and 18K = 75.000%. This is the pure-metal content used for valuation; it is not a hallmark tolerance (see 15 U.S.C. 295 for that).
