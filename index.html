Rule 0 No hallucinated prices. No invented numbers. Ever.

Every price, karat and gram weight on this site was read from the seller's own product page and stamped with the date it was read. The short version

What this site does

Collects ring listings from sellers' own sites, records the karat, the stated gold weight, the price and the return terms with the exact quote and the date, then computes what the gold is actually worth at the current spot price so an inflated or suspiciously cheap price is obvious.

Open the ring list →

What this site refuses to do

It will not estimate a weight from a photo, will not repeat a price from a search result, will not call an unmarked ring “solid gold”, and will not fill a blank with a guess. If you see a number here, it was on the seller's page when the page was opened.

The checklist this project is built on

Each line is either enforced by U.S. law, or is a rule of thumb that the dataset makes checkable. Where a rule comes from law, the citation is linked.

  • ✓
    Gold purity. Confirm the karat — 10K, 14K, 18K. Under 16 CFR § 23.3 the word “gold” must be preceded by the karat.
  • ✓
    Solid gold. The listing must say solid gold. Vermeil, gold plate, gold filled and gold overlay all have legal definitions that mean “not solid” (16 CFR § 23.4, § 23.3(c)).
  • ✓
    Hallmark. A karat stamp plus a maker's/dealer's mark. The marking side of this is U.S. law: 15 U.S.C. § 297(b).
  • ✓
    Weight. The gram weight, not the carat weight of the stone. A legitimate seller can provide it.
  • ✓
    Seller reputation. Independent reviews, complaints, and real contact details on the seller's own site.
  • ✓
    Price vs gold. Compare the price to the ring's actual gold content at the current spot price — the calculator on this site does exactly that.
  • ✓
    Return policy. Covering the item after you inspect it. Watch for exclusions on resized, engraved or custom pieces.
  • ✓
    Payment. Credit card, for chargeback protection. Avoid irreversible payment methods.
  • ✓
    Documentation. Keep the listing, receipt, description and every purity claim the seller made.
  • ✓
    On arrival. Independent jeweller first, wearing/resizing second.
  • ✓
    Best verification. XRF or another professional purity test. A stamp is a claim; an assay is evidence.
  • ✓
    Walk away. If the seller will not let you independently verify and return it, do not buy.
