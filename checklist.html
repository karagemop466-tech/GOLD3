Zero-hallucination policy

Every price, purity claim, weight and policy on this site is copied from a named source page and stamped with the date it was read. If a source does not publish a value, the value is left blank and marked unverified - it is never estimated, inferred, or filled in to make a row look complete. Prices move daily: always re-check the linked page before buying.

The twelve-point buying checklist

Each test is tied to the law or to the retailer's own published policy, and each one is applied to every listing in the tables. The point is not to trust a stamp - it is to make the seller commit to a fact in writing, then verify it independently.

1

Gold purity

Confirm the karat - 10K, 14K, 18K - and that the number is attached to the word gold on the listing itself.

Why: 16 CFR 23.3(c)(1): a fineness designation must immediately precede 'Gold' and be equally conspicuous.

How this site checks it: The Purity column records only what the listing states. Where the karat appears only in an image description, the row says so.

2

Solid gold

The listing must explicitly say solid gold - not plated, vermeil, gold-filled, overlay or gold tone.

Why: 16 CFR 23.3(b): surface-plated products must be qualified as plated. 16 CFR 23.4: vermeil is a sterling-silver base.

How this site checks it: The Solid gold column has three states: stated on listing, brand-level only, or not stated. Seven gold listings are 'not stated'.

3

Hallmark

Look for a karat stamp plus a maker's or manufacturer's mark.

Why: 15 U.S.C. 297: a quality mark must be accompanied by the maker's registered trademark or name, applied by the same means and as close as possible.

How this site checks it: No retailer here publishes a hallmark policy, so every row is unverified on this point until you see the ring.

4

Weight

Get the ring's weight in grams from the seller.

Why: Without weight, the gold content cannot be computed - only implied by the price.

How this site checks it: 0 of 17 gold listings publish a weight. Aurate's product feed carries an undocumented numeric 'weight' field that repeats across variants; it is never described as the finished item weight, so it was deliberately not used.

5

Seller reputation

Read independent reviews and complaints; confirm a real phone number or address.

Why: Trustpilot scores are shown with review counts and whether reviews are invited, because sample size changes meaning.

How this site checks it: Retailer cards quote the actual complaint themes, not just the stars.

6

Price

Compare the price with the gold value of the stated weight at the captured spot price.

Why: Gold spot is dated and sourced; the calculator shows the formula.

How this site checks it: Break-even weight is shown for every listing. None prices below the melt value of a plausible ring, so none is 'too cheap'.

7

Return policy

Confirm you can return after inspection - and check the resized/custom exclusions.

Why: Returns are your only remedy if the ring fails assay.

How this site checks it: Blue Nile excludes resized rings and caps free returns at two per year; Aurate allows 15 days; Catbird 20 days plus a $9 fee.

8

Payment

Use a credit card for chargeback protection. Avoid irreversible methods.

Why: Card chargebacks are the practical lever if a seller misrepresents the metal.

How this site checks it: Payment methods are NOT verified per retailer here - that cell is deliberately blank rather than assumed.

9

Documentation

Keep the listing, receipt, description and the seller's purity claims.

Why: You need the claim on record to enforce it later.

How this site checks it: Every row links to the live source page; save it, because listings change.

10

On arrival

Before wearing or resizing, have an independent jeweller verify purity and weight.

Why: Resizing voids the return right at several retailers - verify first.

How this site checks it: See the return windows above and book the appointment before delivery.

11

Best verification

Ask for XRF testing or another professional assay - do not rely on the stamp.

Why: 15 U.S.C. 295 allows a tolerance between the mark and the actual fineness, so the stamp is a claim within a tolerance, not proof.

How this site checks it: No listing here includes an assay report; that step is yours, after delivery.

12

Walk away

If the seller will not let you independently verify or return it, do not buy.

Why: A seller who blocks verification has told you the answer.

How this site checks it: The Flagged page records every listing where a key fact is missing or contradictory.

The one-sentence version

A 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is that purity - a reputable seller, a proper mark with a maker's mark, a sensible price and an independent assay after delivery are what actually protect you.

Red flags that should end the conversation

  • The listing will not use the word solid, or answers with "gold" / "real gold" instead of a karat.
  • No weight in grams, and refusal to provide one.
  • A price far below the melt value of a plausible ring weight (the calculator will show this).
  • Returns excluded on resized, engraved or custom items - combined with pressure to resize immediately.
  • Payment by wire, crypto, gift card or friends-and-family transfer.
  • Reluctance to name the manufacturer or show the inside of the band.
