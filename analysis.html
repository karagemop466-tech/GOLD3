Zero-hallucination policy — No Hallucinations Commitment

No hallucinations: every price, purity claim, weight and policy on this site is copied from a named official source page and stamped with the date it was read. If a source does not publish a value, the value is left blank and marked unverified — it is never estimated, inferred, or filled in to make a row look complete. 1. The requirement that matters most is the one nobody publishes

No seller in this dataset publishes a per-item XRF scan or an independent assay. The karat is always the seller's own claim, at best backed by a stamp. That is exactly the gap the checklist warns about: a 14K or 18K stamp does not prove the ring is that purity. Independent testing on arrival is the only step that does.

2. “18k” can mean a coating

2 rows here carry “18k” in the product name while being vermeil: a gold layer over sterling silver, which 16 CFR 23.4 defines and names. A shopper scanning for “18k” would find it and could easily misread what they were buying.

3. A headline price may be the setting only

Several major-retailer rows are “Setting Price” or “Starting from”: the centre diamond is bought separately. Comparing those to a complete ring's price is a category error, so the basis is printed under every price.

4. Weights exist, but never unqualified

14 of 31 rows publish grams, and every one of them qualifies the figure: approximate, ±5%, stated for a reference size, or shared across two metals. A melt figure from any of them is an estimate, not a measurement.

Melt value versus asking price

Fine gold content = published weight × karat ÷ 24. Melt value = fine grams × $129.32/g. The multiple is what you pay divided by the raw gold in the ring. It is not a fair-price verdict: labour, setting, stone and margin are real costs.

5. The return right is where deals actually fail

Return terms are the most common outright failure in this dataset: one policy excludes precious metals entirely, one product page overrides its brand's 30-day policy with a non-returnable, exchange-only statement, and several windows collapse if the item is resized or engraved. Read the seller register before comparing prices.
