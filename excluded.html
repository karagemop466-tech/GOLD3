Zero-hallucination policy

Every price, purity claim, weight and policy on this site is copied from a named source page and stamped with the date it was read. If a source does not publish a value, the value is left blank and marked unverified - it is never estimated, inferred, or filled in to make a row look complete. Prices move daily: always re-check the linked page before buying.

Excluded: not solid gold

These are the look-alikes. They are listed so the filter is visible and testable - not because they are bad jewellery, but because they fail the requirement this project exists to enforce: the metal must be solid gold throughout.

Why vermeil fails the test: 16 CFR 23.4 defines vermeil as a sterling silver base coated with gold of at least 10K and 2½ microns thick. The gold is a coating, not the body of the ring, so there is no meaningful "gold weight" to calculate. Source: 16 CFR 23.4.

The same design, three metals

Mejuri sells a ring called the Dôme Figure Ring in Sterling Silver at $138 and in 18k Gold Vermeil at $158 - and Mejuri also sells solid-gold rings under other names. A familiar product name tells you nothing about the metal; the material selector does. This is why every row in this dataset records the exact option selected, not just the product name.

Aurate likewise sells three rates - "Vermeil, 14-karat solid gold and 18-karat solid gold" - on the same site. Always check which rate you have selected before reading the price.

What to say instead

If you want the look at a lower price, that is a legitimate choice - but it is a different purchase with different economics. Vermeil cannot be sold for its gold content, does not accumulate value, and cannot be assayed to a karat the way a solid alloy can. Buy it because you like it, not because it is "basically gold".
