CHK-01 Gold Purity Confirm the karat — 10K, 14K, 18K, etc. Higher karat = more gold. Under FTC 16 CFR § 23.3(b)–(c), unqualified 'Gold' means 24K fine gold throughout; alloys below 24K must immediately precede 'Gold' with an equally conspicuous karat fineness (e.g., 10K = 41.67%, 14K = 58.33%, 18K = 75.00%). Under 15 U.S.C. § 295, permissible downward assay tolerance is 3 parts per 1,000 without solder and 7 parts per 1,000 with solder. FTC 16 CFR § 23.3 & 15 U.S.C. § 295

CHK-03 Hallmark & Maker's Mark Look for a 10K/14K/18K stamp plus a maker's/manufacturer's mark. CHK-07 Return Policy & Custom/Resized Exclusions Make sure you can return it after receiving and inspecting it. Check specifically for exclusions on resized/custom rings. Online buyers need an unconditional inspection return window (typically 14–30+ days in unworn condition with original tags/certificates) sufficient to visit an independent jeweler for non-destructive testing. Resized, engraved, or made-to-order custom rings frequently void return eligibility or incur restocking fees — verify exact exclusions prior to purchase. Seller Contractual Return Terms & FTC FCBA

CHK-08 Payment Protection Prefer a credit card for purchase protection. Avoid irreversible payment methods. Under the federal Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA, 15 U.S.C. § 1666), credit card buyers can dispute billing errors in writing within 60 days — including goods not delivered as agreed (e.g., counterfeit/plated ring misrepresented as solid karat gold) — and withhold payment on the disputed amount during investigation. Wire transfers, crypto, cash apps, and bank transfers lack FCBA chargeback rights. Fair Credit Billing Act (FTC Guidance)

CHK-10 Post-Arrival Independent Jeweler Verification Upon arrival: Before wearing/resizing it, take it to an independent jeweler and ask them to verify the gold purity and weight. Never remove security tags, wear, engrave, or resize the ring before independent verification, because alteration voids retailer return policies. An independent jeweler or appraiser with calibrated gram/milligram scales and non-destructive testing equipment can confirm gross weight, hallmark/maker's mark legibility, and alloy fineness while keeping the item in returnable condition. 16 CFR § 23.0(b) Note (Appraisers) & NIST HB 130

CHK-11 Professional XRF Purity Testing Best verification: Ask for XRF testing or another professional gold-purity test; don't rely solely on the stamp. ISO 23345:2021 (confirmed 2026-09-03) standardizes non-destructive precious metal fineness confirmation of finished jewelry by Energy-Dispersive X-Ray Fluorescence (ED-XRF). Importantly, ISO 23345:2021 explicitly notes ED-XRF measures surface layers (~10–20 microns) and 'is not suitable for any coated items' alone — pair XRF elemental readout with exact weight/density checks (and note ISO 11426:2021 cupellation fire assay is the destructive referee standard). ISO 23345:2021 & ISO 11426:2021

CHK-12 Walk-Away Rule & Stamp Skepticism If the seller won't let you independently verify/return it: Don't buy it. A 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove purity. Counterfeit 14K/18K/750 metal stamps are inexpensive and commonly applied to brass, silver, or electroplated rings. Because destructive fire assay (ISO 11426:2021) cannot be performed on a ring you intend to wear, buyer safety requires all four pillars together: (1) reputable verifiable seller, (2) compliant quality + maker's mark (15 U.S.C. § 297), (3) economically plausible price vs. gold weight, and (4) unconditional return window allowing post-delivery XRF + weight verification.
